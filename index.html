Communication and Cooperation in Economic Decision Making: An Experimental Investigation in Threshold Public Goods Games

Elbakyan, T.

BA thesis, Manoogian Simone College of Business and Economics, American University of Armenia. Supervisor: V. Baghdasaryan.

PDF Download 32 pp.

This paper examines whether pre-play cheap talk improves coordination in a threshold public goods game, and whether the richness of the communication channel determines the size of the effect. In a laboratory experiment with 135 undergraduates over 13 rounds, unrestricted chat increased average contributions by 0.45 coins relative to a no-communication baseline of 5.93 (p = 0.009), while a numerical intention signal decreased them by 0.56 coins (p = 0.001) and reduced payoffs by 1.47 coins per round. Within the signalling treatment, actual contributions exceeded stated intentions by 0.86 coins (p < 0.001). The effect of communication on cooperation depends critically on its form.Keywords: threshold public goods, cheap talk, communication richness

List Experiment Analysis: Vote Buying in Armenian Elections

Elbakyan, T. and Lalayan, A.

Research report, American University of Armenia. Supervisor: V. Baghdasaryan.

PDF Download 6 pp.

A list experiment (Blair and Imai, 2012) estimates the prevalence of vote-buying acceptance among 264 students at three Yerevan universities. The difference-in-means estimate is 8.65% (SE 0.132; 95% CI −17.3% to 34.6%). Variance and floor/ceiling diagnostics show no clear design effects. A power analysis finds that detecting a true prevalence of 10% at 80% power would require about 3,600 respondents.Keywords: list experiment, vote buying, sensitive questions, power analysis

Cooperation and Strategic Behavior in an Ultimatum Game: Evidence from an Experiment with CS and BAB Students

Elbakyan, T. and Margarian, L.

Department of Economics, American University of Armenia. Supervisor: V. Baghdasaryan.

PDF Download 8 pp.

A one-shot ultimatum game, implemented in oTree with 40 students, tests whether academic background shapes fairness preferences. Computer Science responders accepted every positive offer, consistent with the subgame-perfect prediction. Business Administration responders rejected offers they perceived as unfair, including 400/600 splits, in line with inequality aversion.Keywords: ultimatum game, fairness, strategic behavior

Economic and Demographic Determinants of Birth Rates Across U.S. States: An Econometric Analysis

Elbakyan, T. and Barseghyan, D.

Department of Economics, American University of Armenia. Supervisor: N. Ohanyan.

PDF Download 12 pp.

Using state-level data, this paper models birth rates among women aged 15–49 as a function of housing costs, income, age, education, insurance coverage and marriage age. After addressing multicollinearity and testing for heteroskedasticity, and comparing OLS, robust and GLS estimates, the final model (adjusted R² = 0.713, n = 150) identifies housing costs, average age, income and health insurance coverage as significant determinants.Keywords: fertility, econometrics, OLS, GLS, multicollinearity

Addressing the Affordable Housing Crisis in Armenia

Elbakyan, T.

Philosophy, Politics and Economics Seminar, American University of Armenia. Supervisor: A. Balayan.

PDF Download 9 pp.

Rents in Yerevan have risen faster than incomes, raising the rent-to-income ratio from 28% to 38% between 2019 and 2023. This paper examines the demographic, economic and legal drivers of the crisis, including conflict-driven migration, speculative investment and gaps in the 2021 property tax reform, and proposes a National Affordable Housing Strategy built on vacancy charges, inclusionary zoning and stronger tenant protection.Keywords: affordable housing, property tax, inclusionary zoning, Armenia
